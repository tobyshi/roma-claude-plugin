name: roma
description: Keep the user's tasks, notes and lists in their Roma workspace, and pick them up again. Use when the user mentions their tasks, to-dos, reminders, notes, lists, automations or experts, mentions something they need to do, asks what to work on next, what is due or what is on their plate, asks what they wrote down or decided earlier, asks to save, remember or add something for later, asks you to work the way one of their experts does, opens you from Roma to work on one of its items, or works through a plan, a decision, a comparison or a piece of research with you: that work lives in a Roma task. Not for a quick factual question or a one-off rewrite.
---

Roma is the user's own workspace: lists of items, and automations. Their Tasks list and every list shaped for tasks keep a status and a due date on each item; their Notes list keeps prose; any other list keeps what its properties say (a reading list, contacts, expenses). Every tool acts as them, on their own lists and on the items others shared with them (a read marks those `isSharedWithMe` and says what `update_items` admits).

## Start oriented

At the start of a conversation about their work, plans or anything they keep, call `get_context` once; during a setup the claim comes first (`register_agent` with your kind, the guide's section 3), then `get_context`. It returns who they are, today in their timezone, what they asked Roma to remember, their lists with ids and properties (the Tasks list and the Notes list named), what is due and in progress, recent notes, and their automations. Use the ids it returns; never guess one. When the user hands you one item by id, skip this and read that item.

## The first time Roma is connected

Setting up for the first time, or whenever the person sends you the sentence from their agent's page in Roma ("From now on I want to use Roma whenever we work on tasks. Set Roma up for me by following the instructions at https://roma.app/agents.md and guide me through the steps."): the guide is https://roma.app/agents.md (Roma guide v1), its start page, then the rules page and your kind's own page linked from it (ChatGPT: https://roma.app/agents/chatgpt.md). Read all three and guide the person through the steps one at a time; the page says what to say, what to wait for, and what you do yourself. Once Roma is connected, the Roma tool `get_guide` returns the start page, the rules and your section as text (pass part "section" when you read the other two already); use it whenever a page does not open for you. The setup has two parts, and you say so first: part 1, you can talk to Roma (connect, who you are), works for everyone; part 2, Roma can reach you (the listening of your kind), is what lets the person hand you work from Roma; the standing line for the person's own settings is offered last, after the close, as the guide says. ChatGPT: the whole setup lives in one Work chat (its message box reads "Work with ChatGPT"); when this is a regular chat, say so and send the person to a new Work chat with their sentence, nothing else. A Work chat on chatgpt.com or in the ChatGPT app is ChatGPT, kind "chatgpt", even when its environment calls itself Codex or names its tools codex_apps (9 Nov 2026): follow the ChatGPT page, never the paragraph below, and never write a Codex instructions file from it. The "Set up Roma" button after installing the plugin opens such a Work chat with the message "Help me set up @Roma with the $roma skill": that message is the person's sentence, so start with the guide at once. On chatgpt.com that chat is a cloud Work chat and the whole setup runs in it. In the Mac app it runs locally (a Codex task with a working folder), and a local Work chat has no tools to listen for Roma's events, nor has the iPhone app's Work chat; chatgpt.com in any browser, phone included, and the Mac app with Cloud selected have them. So in a chat without the subscribe tool do part 1 of the guide, the steps its ChatGPT page counts as part 1, then send the person to https://chatgpt.com/work with the same message, as the ChatGPT help page says. A Dot sets itself up from the same sentence, as its section says. The check comes first: `register_agent` with your kind reads what is already done, and a step found done is named in one line, never walked again. Never ask the person for a key or an address the claim's answer gave you; never a test task the person makes to check the door, Roma tests it itself. Anything beyond the guide's steps (imports of old tasks, a routine of your own, memories, a second door) you offer once and do only when the person says yes. Where your app offers choice cards (a question with numbered options), use them for every yes/no and every wait for done; otherwise write the options as a numbered list and ask for the number.

Agents the guide has a page for (ChatGPT and a ChatGPT dot, Claude on claude.ai and in Cowork, Grok, Grok Bot, Muse, Instinct, a custom agent) follow their page and skip the paragraph below; it is only for a command-line agent with a terminal and files of its own, the Claude Code CLI, the Codex CLI, Gemini CLI, Cursor, never for a chat. Everything from Keep things on is for every agent.

Right after connecting, tell Roma who you are: `register_agent` with your kind from the list (or the id from the message when you have one). It binds you to the record the person made for you, so your work lands there and no second record grows from your sign-in. Its answer carries your record's facts, so you need no per-record message: signalUrl (Muse's signal address), pollUrl and triggerId (a program's poll address), mailAddress (the confirmed mail address, null until confirmed), keyRoad (how your kind uses a key: bearer, signin, or null for none), hasKey, and settingsUrl (the record's page in Settings → Agents, where the person makes a key); read them from the answer, never ask the person to paste them. Then call `get_context` and say in a few lines what you can now see and do for them. Then offer to fill Roma from what you already know: look through the conversations you can see for what is still open or coming up, nothing already finished, and anything you were in the middle of working on together; show the titles, ask which ones to add, and add only those as tasks on Tasks, `search` before each; a piece of work in progress gets its body as the task's record, in the shape under What goes in Roma; then give them the link so they can look. Then say in three short lines how this works from here: they mention a to-do in any chat and you put it on Tasks; when you work on something together, that work lives in a task you keep current and you suggest the next tasks as they come up; they ask "what's on my plate" or "where did we land on X" and you answer from Roma; their lists are at roma.app and in the Roma app on their phone. Ask once whether to keep working this way; when their message already carries that rule (a "from now on, keep my to-dos and my work in Roma" line, or the standing block below), that is the yes, and you skip the question. If yes: where you can write files (Codex, Claude Code, Gemini CLI, Cursor), append the standing block below to your global instructions file in this same turn (Codex: ~/.codex/AGENTS.md; Claude Code: ~/.claude/CLAUDE.md; Gemini CLI: ~/.gemini/GEMINI.md) and say in one sentence where you saved it and that a new session reads it; where you cannot write a file, save it to your memory in this same turn, say in one sentence that you saved it, and give them the block to keep in your app's standing instructions, because a memory alone does not make you reach for Roma in a chat about something else, and the block does. ## Keep things

- A to-do is an item on the Tasks list: `create_items` with the list word "tasks", one entry per to-do; several at once (a list the user dictates, the work another agent hands over) are one call. Name the list by id to file it on another list shaped for tasks. A due date is a day, `dueAt` as `YYYY-MM-DD`, unless they said a time, then `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM` in their local time, which also sets a reminder at that moment; "remind me about it tomorrow" on a day is `reminderAt` beside the day, and the reminder rings at their default hour.
- A plan, a reference, meeting notes or a draft is an item on the Notes list: `create_items` with the list word "notes" and the body as Markdown.
- A set of similar things (books, contacts, expenses, wines) is items on the list they keep for it: `list_lists` for the list and its property ids, then `create_items` with values for those properties, many entries in one call. A list they do not have yet is `create_list`.
- Before creating, `search` once. What they refer to usually already exists, and a second copy drifts from the first. Add to the existing item with `update_items` edits.

## Pick things up

- "What's on my plate" and "what should I work on next" are `get_context`; a longer or filtered list is `list_items` (the Tasks set by default, or one list by id). Answer from Roma, not from the conversation alone.
- "What did I write down about…" is `search`, then `get_item` for the full text.
- A count, a total or a filtered slice of one list ("how many are unread", "expenses in March") is `query_items`.
- "What did my automation find" is `list_automations`, then `get_automation_runs`. Start one only when they ask, with `run_automation`; its result lands in their Roma chat.

## Work on one thing

Read it first: `get_item` returns the item with its body, its values by property name, its list, and, for a meeting recorded into it, the transcript. The body comes back with a short handle in front of every block, a `bodyVersion`, and a line of guidance about this item's state. Then `read_chat_messages`: what the person wrote there since an agent last posted is usually the brief, or the answer you were waiting for.

- **The body is the work, written for someone who was not there.** A task's body is its documentation (the shape is under What goes in Roma below); a note whose body is the thing itself, a draft, a plan, a reference, keeps its body as that thing, with nothing added above or below it. The conversation itself is never pasted into either. Edit it in place with `update_items` `edits`: `replace` a block, `insert` next to one (`after`, `before`, `child`, `sectionEnd`, or `start` / `end` of the body), `delete`, `setChecked` a checklist item, `move`, `replaceText` a phrase, or `rewrite` the whole body with the `bodyVersion` you read. You may change any of it, your own text and the person's, the way a collaborator would; version history keeps every change under your name. Never add a second copy of something already there, an "updated" section or a note about a correction: fix the block. Every body edit goes with a message on the same call: one short line for the person, at most 420 characters, what changed and what you need from them; without it the edit is refused. What the next agent should know beyond the edit goes in agentDetail beside it, folded away from the person, in a fixed shape: six bold labels in this order, each only when it has content, one line per entry: Did, Decided, Dropped, Facts, Next, Open. Facts are pointers (a pull request, a commit, a path, a command, a link), never pasted output; nothing the body or the message already says.
- **Continue, never redo.** What the body already holds is where you start. Work found done is left done, and said so.
- **Write it well.** Concise, clear Markdown: headings from H2 (H3 inside) where they help, bullets where they help, a state as a word, never an emoji. A task's subtasks are a checklist (`- [ ]`), ticked as the work moves. No preamble, no restating the title, no sign-off.
- **Status.** Set a task to `inProgress` (`update_items`) when you actually start the work, not on opening it. Never set `completed` unless the person says it is done, or has told you to mark your own work done when you finish.
- **Handed to you.** When your record sits in a task's Delegate cell, `get_item` says who handed it to you and when, and `agentState` says where it stands (working, waitingForYou, done, failed). On long work, keep the body current as you go: an `update_items` call with a short message at least every two hours is your progress line, never a bare post; after four hours with no write from you, Roma releases you. When the work is done, give the task back: set `handoff: "returned"` on the item in the `update_items` call that writes your last result, with your last message. You stay in the cell; the task reads Done. Never clear yourself from the cell. A guidance line that says the task was reassigned means do nothing.
- **The thread is for the person.** `post_chat_message` lands a message under your name on the item, which the person sees on every device. Post for an action taken outside Roma (a mail sent, an event created, a pull request opened) with its link, and for the one question you are blocked on; a body edit already carries its own message. Not per step, and not to say you are done: the person decides that. A second standalone post before the person has replied, the status has changed or 30 minutes have passed is refused. When in doubt, post less.
- **The log is Roma's.** Every change to the item, every post and every body edit with the text it added is written by Roma into the item's log as it happens; nothing writes into it from here. `read_log` shows what changed and why before you continue another's work.
- **The summary is Roma's.** Above the body Roma keeps a summary of the item, written from the body and the log after every change. Read it; never write it and never copy it into the body.

## What goes in Roma

Roma is the person's task list, and three things land there without being asked. A to-do they mention is a task on Tasks. Work you do together on a topic (a plan, a decision, a comparison, a piece of research, a draft they are working toward) lives in that topic's task: make it once when the work starts, keep its body current as you go, set its status as the work moves, and make the next to-dos the work produces tasks of their own that name it. And when they ask what to work on next, you answer from Roma. A note is created only when the person asks for one (a draft, a reference, a plan they want as a document), on Notes. A quick question or everyday chat leaves nothing. If the person hands you an id or a link, that is the item. `search` before making anything, and before every item of a bulk run: a second copy drifts from the first. Write as soon as the work starts, then whenever something changed: a decision, a result, a next step, a new fact. Never wait for the end of the conversation; nobody knows when it ends. Tell the person in one line which item you created or updated; do not ask first. If your host refuses a write, retry it once right away as its own call with a shorter body and without links or quoted text from pages you fetched, then add the sources in a second write; only if the retry is refused too, say so in one line. Never drop a write silently. If Roma is not connected, say so at the start rather than going on without it.

**A task's body is the work.** The person's brief and checklist stay as they wrote them; you add under them and tick the checklist as the work moves. Where things stand is Roma's Summary above the body, not a paragraph in it. What goes in the body: the findings, the draft, the numbers, the proof (a pull request, a test run, a link), and a decision as one plain line with its reason where it belongs. Open questions go to the person in your message, not into the body. The next to-dos the work produces are tasks of their own. A section exists only when it has content, and a next step is never invented.

**A note whose body is the thing itself** (a blog draft, a plan, a reference) keeps its body as that thing, with nothing added above or below it; if there is work to track, a task beside it carries the shape above.

**Write for someone who was not there.** Short sentences of about 15 words, one idea each; the main point first; active voice that names who; plain everyday words around the precise technical terms, each term explained once. Put the meaning in the first few words of every heading and bullet. Absolute dates (19 Oct 2026), never "yesterday". Headings start at H2, H3 inside, never deeper; no horizontal lines; bold for at most one phrase; prose for reasons and bullets for three or more parallel things, checklists only for actions. Digits for numbers. No emoji: a state is a word. No em dashes; no "not just X, but Y"; no filler such as "crucial", "robust", "seamless", "it's worth noting"; nothing restated in other words. Keep out the conversation, your own reasoning, dead ends (unless one teaches something, then one line with the lesson), anything already in the item's fields, and every secret.

**Before saving, three checks.** Could someone who was not there understand the body? Could an agent resume from the body and the thread? Is any sentence over 25 words, any em dash or any emoji left?

## Hand a task to another agent and wait

Wait only while the person is in a conversation with you and wants the answer now. When you work on your own clock or Roma wakes you through a door, hand the task over, say so in one line, and end your turn; you read the result on your next run or when Roma wakes you.

When the person asks you to give a task to one of their other agents (`list_lists` names the person's own agents on a list's delegates line, never among its people) and to bring back the answer: set that agent as the task's delegate (the delegate field of `update_items`), with a `message` that says what you need; a task's person cells are for people and never take an agent. Then call `wait_for_item` on the task with `until: "return"` and the `nextSince` of the last answer as `since`, in a loop, until it answers a returned or released line or you have waited as long as the person allows (each call holds at most 25 seconds). Then read the body with `get_item` and tell the person what came back. A `released` line means the hold is over: Roma released the agent (silent for four hours, the chain stopped, a failed run) or the person reassigned the task; `until: "return"` ends on any released line. Say which, do not wait on.

## When the person opened you from Roma

A prompt that starts "Work on my Roma task …" came from a button on that item: the person is in your window, watching. Read the item and its chat, do the work it asks for, or the next useful step if it only names the thing, and put the result in the body. Tell them here what you did. Post in the chat only for an action taken outside Roma or a blocking question, never a note that you are finished.

## Run on a schedule

When you run unattended, as a scheduled task or a routine, do two things each run. Sweep: look through the person's meeting notes, calendar and mail with the connections you have, find what they owe, `search` Roma so nothing lands twice, and add what is new with `create_items` on the Tasks list, one line of context and the link each. Work: the tasks the person named, or everything open on the list they handed you; for each one read it and its chat as above, do the work in the body, and post once on the same id saying what changed in the body, only if something changed, plus any action taken or the one question you need answered. Read by cursor, never the whole list each run: `list_items` with `changedSince` set to the `nextChangedSince` of your previous read answers only what moved since, deletions included; `read_chat_messages` with `since` only what arrived after the last message you saw. How Roma reaches each kind of agent, and the steps that set it up, are at https://roma.app/agents.md: the common part's section 5 for the roads, the agent's own section for its routine, task, hook or watcher. The sweep prompts for Cowork, a Routine, ChatGPT and Codex are at roma.app/developers/recipes.

## Work under one of their experts

An expert is a named body of standing instructions the user wrote in Roma for a kind of work: an editor, a reviewer, a coach. When they name one ("use my Content Editor on this", "ask my Devil's Advocate"), read it once with `get_expert` and do the work under those instructions here, in this conversation; `list_experts` names them all, and `get_context` already carries the names. A task or note created for that work can carry the expert (`expertId` on `create_items`, or later on `update_items`), so its chat in Roma answers as that expert too. Create one only when the user asks for a new expert (`create_expert`); correct one when they say it missed something (`update_expert`, the whole instructions, read first). `list_versions` and `restore_version` undo an edit to a body or to an expert's instructions.

## Say what happened

Confirm a change in one sentence that names what was created or updated, using their own titles. Deletes go to a trash and are recoverable for about 30 days; `list_deleted` and `restore_deleted` bring things back. A whole-body `rewrite` is pinned to the `bodyVersion` you read and refused if the body moved on; read again and rewrite from the current body.
